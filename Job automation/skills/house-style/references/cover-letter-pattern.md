# Cover-Letter Pattern

## Purpose

The letter must explain why this technical problem is the candidate's logical next step. It must complement the resume, not restate it.

## Structure

Write 380-450 words in five paragraphs. Hard maximum 475 words.

1. Role thesis, 55-75 words: identify the role and the specific technical problem that makes it relevant.
2. Primary evidence, 90-110 words: map the strongest recent project to the role's central requirement.
3. Supporting progression, 80-105 words: connect one or two earlier experiences that explain breadth or increasing system depth.
4. Company/research fit, 80-105 words: connect a verified company activity, team problem, method, or research direction to candidate evidence and intended contribution.
5. Contribution close, 30-45 words: state immediate contribution and the logical next step; thank the reader once.

Use a shorter 350-420 word budget when a full recipient-address block is included.

## Sentence style

- Average 18-24 words.
- One main idea per paragraph.
- Alternate concise claims with one longer explanatory sentence.
- Use chronological narrative only when it shows increasing technical depth.
- Use first person, active voice, and concrete engineering verbs.
- Do not bold keywords in the body.

## Tailoring test

Apply the company-swap test: if a paragraph remains valid after replacing the company and role names, it is insufficiently tailored.

Strong company specificity follows:

`verified company/team problem -> candidate evidence -> intended contribution`

Do not praise brand prestige, invent recent news, or claim a relationship with a team or professor without evidence.

## Closing

Reaffirm technical match and contribution. Mention availability or language only when relevant. Do not call the candidate ideal, grateful regardless of outcome, or uniquely suited.
